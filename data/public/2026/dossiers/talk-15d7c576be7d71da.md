## Submitted abstract

I&#39;m a Holistic Health Practitioner and independent researcher on Hawaiʻi Island&#46; This talk brings one open question to the Active Inference community&#58; when a living system learns&#44; does it only move differently through a possibility space that already existed&#44; or can learning reorganize the space itself&#44; changing what becomes available to perceive&#44; generate&#44; or do next&#63;
I come to the question from two directions&#46; The first is physiology&#46; In a 15&#45;month longitudinal study of working first responders&#44; still in design and not yet under review&#44; the question is whether recovery changes which states and actions become available afterward&#44; not only which state a person is in&#46; Nothing has been measured yet&#46; The second is method&#46; In my own cross&#45;domain research process&#44; I carry a relation into another field&#44; try to find where it breaks&#44; and let that information change the next question&#46; The history of that process seems to change which distinctions I can draw next&#46; I present that as method&#44; not as evidence about mechanism&#46;
Same grammar&#44; not yet the same mechanism&#46; Several formal lenses meet here&#58; viability theory and reachable sets work on how the landscape of reachable states is reshaped&#44; the difference between changing what a system generates and what it selects&#44; and recent Active Inference accounts of insight as non&#45;local search&#46;
So the talk ends on a request rather than a result&#58; what observation would distinguish a system moving through a space from a system whose space has changed&#44; and what does Active Inference make of that difference&#63;
