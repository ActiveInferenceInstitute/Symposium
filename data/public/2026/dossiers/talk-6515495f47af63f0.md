# Active inference and design 

Presenter: Luca Possati

Contribution: Pre&#45;recorded Video presentation

Public ID: talk&#45;6515495f47af63f0

## Submitted abstract

Active inference and the free&#45;energy principle transform design theory by providing a unified language for understanding both the production of artifacts and their use&#46; Contemporary design research often separates two problems that are&#44; in practice&#44; deeply entangled&#46; On the one hand&#44; it studies the design process&#58; how designers explore possibilities&#44; negotiate constraints&#44; prototype solutions&#44; and stabilize forms&#46; On the other hand&#44; it studies user&#8211;product interaction&#58; how artifacts orient attention&#44; solicit action&#44; shape habits&#44; and reorganize experience&#46; These two domains are usually described through different vocabularies and evaluated through different metrics&#46;
Active inference offers a way to reconnect them&#46; If living and cognitive systems persist by minimizing uncertainty through perception and action&#44; then design can be understood as the deliberate construction of environments&#44; artifacts&#44; and interfaces that modulate the user&#8217;s field of possible inferences&#46; From this perspective&#44; a product is not simply a passive object that satisfies pre&#45;given needs&#46; It is a predictive structure&#58; a material and semiotic configuration that embodies assumptions about users&#44; contexts&#44; actions&#44; and futures&#46; Conversely&#44; the design process itself can be modeled as the progressive refinement of generative models through which designers attempt to reduce uncertainty about what an artifact should do&#44; how it should appear&#44; and how it will be taken up in practice&#46;
The paper&#8217;s central claim is that active inference gives design a common metric&#58; the modulation of entropy&#44; precision&#44; affordance&#44; and expected free energy across the designer&#8211;artifact&#8211;user system&#46; This makes it possible to describe design as the engineering of predictive niches and Markov blankets that coordinate human and technological agency&#46; The result is a unified framework for analyzing how artifacts are imagined&#44; produced&#44; interpreted&#44; and incorporated into everyday life&#46;
