## Submitted abstract

This Symposium 2026 tutorial covers Active Inference from various angles based on the experience over several years with teaching and applying Active Inference&#46; It begins with a brief descriptive rehearsal of fundamental ideas and principles akin to other previous tutorials to establish a foundation for the audience&#46; However&#44; this will be followed by a tour through various textbook materials and publications&#44; past and present&#44; to emphasize crucial areas for understanding modelling and application areas&#46; This tutorial will include visual and discursive illustrations of a range of model classes for different use&#45;cases&#59; discussions of existing and under&#45;development software libraries&#59; and some frontiers in modelling for identifying directions for further research applications which can be especially valuable for the broader research community in 2026 onward&#46; Topics will include&#44; non&#45;exhaustively&#44; the Bayesian brain hypothesis and behavioralism versus partial observability and modelling internal states&#59; principles of exploration&#44; learning&#44; and novelty&#59; fundamental discrete and continuous models in Active Inference and their relationship&#59; computational psychiatry and its significance to other domains&#59; the FEP and its historical and practical relationship to Bayesian model comparison&#59; software libraries including SPM&#44; pymdp&#44; RxInfer&#44; RGMs&#44; and other developments&#59; hierarchical and hybrid models&#59; and structure learning&#46;
