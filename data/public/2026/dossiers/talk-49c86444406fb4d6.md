# Adaptive Prosociality Through Allostatic Regulation in Social Dynamics&#58; A Simulation Study

Presenter: Hyojun Choi

Contribution: Pre&#45;recorded Video presentation

Public ID: talk&#45;49c86444406fb4d6

## Submitted abstract

How can a prosocial agent reconcile self&#45;protection with convergence toward mutual cooperation&#63; Classic reactive strategies&#44; such as Tit&#45;for&#45;Tat &#40;TFT&#41; and Win&#45;Stay Lose&#45;Shift &#40;WSLS&#41;&#44; perform well only under restrictive conditions&#46; Recent neurocomputational studies show that the brain dynamically updates beliefs over hidden intentions for adaptive social inference&#46; Meanwhile&#44; emerging accounts of social neuroscience suggest that social valuation may be regulated with reference to an internal setpoint&#44; as an agent balances internalized prosocial motives against its current resource state&#46;
Building on both&#44; we propose an active inference agent for the iterated Prisoner&#8217;s Dilemma where prosociality is endogenously regulated&#46; This includes &#40;i&#41; perspective&#45;taking&#44; approximated via particle filter Bayesian inference over the opponent&#8217;s model parameters&#59; &#40;ii&#41; distributional reinforcement learning&#44; implemented as quantile regression temporal difference learning and Dyna planning with generative replay&#59; &#40;iii&#41; prosociality regulator&#44; which dynamically controls the weight placed on the other&#8217;s expected utility relative to a setpoint&#59; and &#40;iv&#41; a self&#45;model&#44; which memorizes partner&#45;specific social expectations and learned reward distributions across interactions&#46;
In preliminary simulations&#44; our agent robustly tracks fluctuating opponent intentions and differentiates reciprocating from exploitative partners&#46; Under non&#45;stationary payoffs in mixed memory&#45;one populations&#44; it achieves higher long&#45;run average payoffs than any other tested strategy after a learning phase&#44; while maintaining mutual cooperation at a level above TFT and WSLS yet below ALLC and GTFT&#46; These findings highlight setpoint&#45;referenced regulation as a candidate computational foundation for adaptive prosociality&#44; offering mechanistic insights into how self&#45;protection and mutual cooperation can be jointly realized and an extensible blueprint for socially aligned embodied agents&#46;
