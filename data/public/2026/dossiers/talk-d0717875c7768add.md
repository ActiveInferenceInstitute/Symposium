# Dynamic Action&#45;Space Adaptation via Contrastive Transition Adaptation in Active Inference

Presenter: Tran Hoang Linh

Contribution: Pre&#45;recorded Video presentation

Public ID: talk&#45;d0717875c7768add

## Submitted abstract

Active Inference commonly assumes a fixed&#44; pre&#45;specified action space and sufficiently informative preferences for planning&#46; In realistic settings&#44; however&#44; an agent may encounter previously unavailable actions while receiving only sparse goal information and operating with a short planning horizon&#46; Existing Active Inference formulations generally assume transition models for a predefined action set&#44; leaving the initialization of newly introduced actions without direct transition evidence underexplored&#46; Moreover&#44; while Dirichlet transition learning provides a principled mechanism for accumulating observed evidence&#44; it primarily reinforces supported transitions and does not explicitly exploit counter&#45;evidence indicating that candidate transitions are inconsistent with observed outcomes&#46; We propose Dynamic Action&#45;Space Adaptation &#40;DASA&#41;&#44; a framework that enables Active Inference agents to incorporate new actions during interaction&#44; initialize each new action&#8217;s transition model using the average transition slice of existing actions&#44; and progressively refine it through Contrastive Transition Adaptation &#40;CTA&#41; as evidence accumulates&#46; At the core of DASA&#44; CTA combines complementary positive and negative updates for newly introduced actions&#46; Positive updates adapt the reinforcement magnitude according to the current transition belief&#44; assigning stronger updates to low&#45;probability transitions supported by evidence while limiting excessive reinforcement of already confident transitions&#46; Negative updates explicitly reduce support for candidate transitions contradicted by observed evidence&#44; enabling the transition model to adapt in both supportive and corrective directions&#46; Across eight random seeds and two target directions&#44; CTA achieved lower MAE than Dirichlet transition learning in all runs&#44; reducing the mean error from 0&#46;1581 to 0&#46;1370 &#40;13&#46;4&#37;&#41;&#44; while also achieving a slightly lower mean RMSE &#40;0&#46;3006 vs 0&#46;3030&#41;&#46; This indicates more consistent overall recovery of the ground&#45;truth transition dynamics&#44; despite seed&#45;dependent variation in larger localized errors&#46; Overall&#44; DASA provides a practical mechanism for incorporating and adapting newly introduced actions in Active Inference&#44; allowing their transition models to become progressively better aligned with the underlying environment dynamics&#46;
