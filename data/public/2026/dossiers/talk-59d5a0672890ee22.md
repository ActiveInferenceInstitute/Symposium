# Language&#44; Self and the Shadow Audience

Presenter: Robert Worden

Contribution: Pre&#45;recorded Video presentation

## Submitted abstract

The highly developed human sense of self is one of our most distinctive traits&#46; We each acquire  our sense of self in conversations&#44; as we learn how conversational partners regard us&#46; Our primary sense of self is not &#8216;myself as I experience myself&#8217;&#44; but &#8216;myself as I think other people see me&#8217; &#8211; a second&#45;hand view of myself&#46; In private verbal thought&#44; with nobody else physically present&#44; other people are represented as a shadow audience in our minds &#8211; an audience which sees what we do or say&#44; and judges us for it&#46; The shadow audience is the source of human emotion and motivation&#46;
 
Language started in conversational pragmatics&#44; before there was complex syntax or semantics&#46; Pragmatics requires us to mind&#45;read&#44; to infer what a conversational partner thinks&#44; wants or needs&#46; In this way we infer what or conversational partners think about us &#8211; how they assess our social status&#46; In any conversation&#44; we are concerned about our own social status in the eyes of a conversational partner&#44; or of a shadow audience&#46; This is illustrated by the pragmatic phenomenon of conversational irony&#46;
