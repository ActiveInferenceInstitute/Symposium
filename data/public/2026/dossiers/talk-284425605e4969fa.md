## Submitted abstract

Active inference frames biological and cognitive agency as the imperatives of self&#45;evidencing&#8212;maintaining systemic integrity by minimising variational free energy within an open&#44; stochastic environment&#46; Under this formalism&#44; agents&#44; from single cells to complex organisms&#44; persist by regulating their internal states against entropic dissipation&#44; enacting a Markov blanket that partitions self from world&#46; This imperative entails sophisticated inference&#44; balancing epistemic drives against pragmatic preferences&#44; thereby constituting adaptive agency&#46;
This talk extends this foundational picture of selfhood to the domain of political communities and global justice theory&#44; demonstrating that the same dynamical constraints that govern individual persistence scale hierarchically to structure collective organisation and inter&#45;state conduct&#46; Drawing on the Free Energy Principle&#44; political communities are modelled as open&#44; self&#45;organising systems embedded within a nested hierarchy of interacting Markov blankets&#46; Their stability is not intrinsic but relational&#44; contingent upon continuous exchange of information&#44; resources&#44; and institutional constraints with their environment&#46;
Within this multiscale architecture&#44; global justice emerges as an active regulatory imperative&#58; a homeostatic constraint that maintains a nonequilibrium steady state across coupled societies&#46; We formalise this through the expected free energy decomposition&#44; where just policies balance risk&#8212;adherence to shared normative constraints such as human rights and non&#45;aggression&#8212;against ambiguity&#8212;the active resolution of uncertainty arising from structural interdependence&#46;
The normative implications are acute&#46; We identify a pervasive pathology of self&#45;organisation&#44; wherein dominant or insular political regimes mis&#45;specify themselves as closed systems&#46; By suppressing external coupling and attenuating sensory evidence&#44; they optimise local coherence at the expense of systemic stability&#46; Drawing parallels to oncogenic cellular proliferation and psychotic cognitive inference&#44; we argue that such closure systematically underestimates global risk and propagates destabilising volatility&#46; Ultimately&#44; we contend that justice is not a static distributive outcome nor a single&#45;level contractual procedure&#44; but the dynamic property of a multiscale system that successfully synchronises its trajectories under persistent uncertainty&#46; This offers a unified&#44; first&#45;principles framework for linking the philosophy of agency to the normative demands of a deeply interconnected global order&#46;
