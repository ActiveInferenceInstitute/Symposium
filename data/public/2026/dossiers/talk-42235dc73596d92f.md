# Solving the Relevance Problem&#58; A General Relevance Criterion in Active Inference

Presenter: Montijn van Loo

Contribution: Pre&#45;recorded Video presentation

## Submitted abstract

This paper proposes a simpler account of generalized relevance realization within active inference&#44; responding to challenges to existing approaches by specifying an explicit relevance&#45;selection criterion&#46; The generalized relevance problem concerns how agents identify information and action consequences worth considering without exhaustive examination&#46; The proposed criterion is that a recognized state becomes relevant when it activates a learned prediction of an outcome that would satisfy or frustrate an existing preference&#46; Because such predictions may concern actions other than those currently pursued&#44; relevance selection can precede current&#45;policy evaluation&#46; Recruited state information subsequently enters ordinary active&#45;inference policy evaluation&#44; while continuous learning establishes new predictors and predictive relationships&#46; This account explains how learned predictions can recruit relevant information without requiring higher&#45;order preference&#45;precision regulation&#44; while allowing flexible behavior under relatively stable preferences&#46;
