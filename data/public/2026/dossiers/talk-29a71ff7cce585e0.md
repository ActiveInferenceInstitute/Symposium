## Submitted abstract

Repetitive and self&#45;stimulatory behaviors have been re&#45;evaluated over time&#44; evolving from simple dysfunctions to adaptive self&#45;regulatory strategies&#44; however&#44; a unified biological framework capable of explaining their contextual activation and underlying physiology remains incomplete&#46; The present paper proposes a theoretical&#45;hypothetical model &#40;grounded in the Free Energy Principle and the Active Inference framework&#41; that conceptualizes stimming as serving a dual role&#58; both as a temporary homeostatic buffer&#44; activated during states of prefrontal executive overload&#44; and sensorimotor depletion and as a modulator of attentional processes and information processing&#46; Distinctions are delineated between these forms of stimming and the non&#45;creative flow &#40; FNC&#47;NCF&#41; state&#44; alongside specific considerations regarding neurodevelopmental conditions&#46;
