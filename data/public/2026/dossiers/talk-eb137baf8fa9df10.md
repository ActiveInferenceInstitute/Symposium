# Modelling Language Comprehension with Embodied Active Inference

Presenter: Vitória de Souza

Contribution: Pre&#45;recorded Video presentation

## Submitted abstract

How do we understand sentences like &#8220;The boy is opening a can of soda&#8221;&#63; Accounts of grounded and embodied cognition propose that comprehension is supported by the partial reactivation of sensorimotor states&#46; For instance&#44; understanding the &#8220;The boy is opening a can of soda&#8221; entails reactivating motor and visual perceptual states congruent with the action described &#40;i&#46;e&#46;&#44; pinching&#41;&#46; While verbal theories have been offered to account for sensorimotor contributions to comprehension&#44; mathematical and mechanistic models are scant&#46; To address this gap&#44; this study provides a proof&#45;of&#45;concept&#44; hierarchical active inference model of sensorimotor contributions to sentence comprehension&#46; In our study&#44; we simulate sensorimotor simulations in a seminal sentence&#45;picture verification task in which participants read a sentence and then are shown a picture of an agent performing an action that either matches or mismatches the shape of the action implied by the sentence&#46; Behaviourally&#44; participants are slower at deciding that the picture reflects the concepts of the sentence when the shape mismatches&#44; implicating sensorimotor reactivation&#46; Our model simulations show that mismatching shapes cause extended epistemic foraging&#44; leading to slower reaction times&#46; This model provides a mathematical and mechanistic account of embodied cognition&#44; extending verbal theories&#46;
