# Unlocking Robotic Dexterity

Presenter: Matthew Brown

Contribution: Pre&#45;recorded Video presentation

Public ID: talk&#45;36b60916ca76bc85

## Submitted abstract

Most real&#45;world work is messy&#44; variable&#44; and full of contact&#58; cables that flex&#44; parts that don&#8217;t quite fit&#44; valves that stick&#46; Traditional robots excel only where conditions are controlled and repeatable&#44; and today&#8217;s leading AI approaches &#40;vision&#45;language&#45;action models&#44; diffusion policies&#44; and embodied foundation models&#41; struggle to close the gap&#46; They depend on massive datasets that don&#8217;t exist for physical interaction&#44; require heavy compute at inference time&#44; and break down when reality drifts from their training distribution&#46;
This talk argues that dexterity is not primarily a data problem but an adaptation problem&#46; Nature doesn&#8217;t solve manipulation by training once offline and hoping it generalizes&#46; It learns continuously at the point of contact&#44; with perception&#44; action&#44; and learning operating as a single loop&#46; We contrast the dominant paradigm of universal function approximation&#44; which is retrospective&#44; data&#45;dependent&#44; and time&#45;agnostic&#44; with enactive synchronization&#58; a prospective&#44; time&#45;aware process in which an agent continuously adjusts to maintain predictive coherence with its environment&#46;
Tracing a line from W&#46; Ross Ashby&#8217;s cybernetics and his Homeostat through the Free Energy Principle and Active Inference&#44; we show how ultrastable networks can form a minimum&#45;prediction&#45;error synchronization manifold between a robot and the world it touches&#46; Sensors and motors become the interface through which goals are expressed as preferred states&#44; and behavior emerges from the network&#8217;s drive to stay in equilibrium&#46;
We then show what this looks like in practice&#58; compact models that run on a single CPU at the edge&#44; update in under a millisecond&#44; and learn tasks in minutes rather than weeks&#44; illustrated through new demonstrations of adaptive dexterous manipulation on real robotic hardware&#46;
