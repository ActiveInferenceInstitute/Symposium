# The Mark of the Moral and the Shared Author Hypothesis&#58; An Active Inference Approach to Moral Objectivity

Presenter: Montijn van Loo

Contribution: Pre&#45;recorded Video presentation

Public ID: talk&#45;528af36ab3c9a9d4

## Submitted abstract

Philosophy is characterized by having something to talk about&#44; yet no consensus on how to talk about it&#46; Mathematics helped physics emerge from philosophy into a cumulative science&#46; Active Inference&#44; potentially a unifying theory of psychology&#44; may mark a comparable philosophical turning point&#46; Psychology and sociology are already flourishing in this mathematical direction&#44; but its consequences may reach further&#44; into philosophical problems that have resisted resolution for centuries&#46; This paper applies that prospect to the mark of the moral and the longstanding tension between moral subjectivity and objectivity&#46;
The proposal is that the mark of the moral is the best predicted global outcome under a role&#45;invariant desire&#46; Active Inference represents choice as the evaluation of predicted outcomes through beliefs and desires&#46; Outcomes are relationally represented according to one&#8217;s role within them&#46; An authorial desire is invariant under such role changes&#58; it values the same global outcome equally regardless of which participant&#8217;s perspective is taken&#46; Moral action consequently maximizes expected authorial value&#46;
This distinction clarifies moral disagreement&#46; Individuals may share an authorial desire yet choose differently because their worldviews generate different predictions&#46; Even agreement about immediate consequences need not establish agreement about their wider significance&#46; The Shared Author Hypothesis goes further&#44; proposing that all role&#45;invariant desires are one and the same&#46; Under this hypothesis&#44; moral disagreement ultimately concerns differences in worldview rather than competing moral standards&#46;
The framework thus offers a conditional resolution of the subjectivity&#8211;objectivity problem&#58; moral choices depend on individual beliefs&#44; while their underlying standard of evaluation remains shared&#46; It also redirects moral and political disagreement toward determining which worldview best fits the available evidence&#46;
