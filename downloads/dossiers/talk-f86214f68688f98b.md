# A New Politics&#58; From Linear to Sorted Politics Through the Policy Map

Presenter: Montijn van Loo

Contribution: Livestreamed panel or group session

Public ID: talk&#45;f86214f68688f98b

## Submitted abstract

Political disagreement often reflects competing worldviews&#58; different beliefs about how the world works and what will happen under alternative policies&#46; Yet political debate proceeds linearly&#44; through proposals&#44; responses&#44; and counterresponses&#46; The underlying reasoning and evidence remain fragmented&#44; assumptions can escape scrutiny&#44; and criticism loses its target as debate moves on&#46; Consequently&#44; incompatible worldviews can persist without their disagreements being resolved&#46;
This talk proposes a new politics&#44; motivated by Active Inference&#44; which provides the mathematical object around which political debate can be organized&#58; the policy map&#46; This map expresses a worldview through predicted outcomes under alternative policies&#46; Making it the persistent object of debate allows criticism to retain a stable address rather than disappear in the rhetoric of successive exchanges&#46;
The mathematics of the policy map distinguishes three types of criticism&#58; belief criticism&#44; challenging beliefs about current states or the consequences of policies&#59; outcome incompleteness&#44; identifying omitted consequences&#59; and policy incompleteness&#44; identifying missing alternatives&#46; These provide the horizontal sorting of political debate&#46;
Vertical sorting organizes the reasoning beneath each prediction&#44; allowing criticism to reach its underlying assumptions and evidence&#46; Unresolved objections remain visible until answered or incorporated through revision&#44; potentially changing the predicted outcomes and preferred policies&#46;
In this way&#44; Active Inference offers a normative application that goes beyond describing politics as if political actors performed Bayesian inference&#46; It motivates transforming the very structure of political debate&#44; replacing rhetorical exchanges with cumulative criticism and incentivizing a shared search for the worldview that best fits the available evidence&#46;
The ultimate aim is a politics in which criticism can actually bite&#44; transforming not only political debates&#44; but the world shaped by its decisions&#46; For the only path forward is critique without exception&#46;
