# When Agency Stalls&#58; The Human Readiness Gap

Presenter: Sheena Yap Chan

Contribution: Pre&#45;recorded Video presentation

Public ID: talk&#45;086ee97e7134b165

## Submitted abstract

Active inference offers a powerful way to understand perception&#44; action&#44; uncertainty&#44; and adaptive behavior&#46; Yet in organizational and social systems&#44; adaptation does not happen through information alone&#46; People must feel ready to update beliefs&#44; reduce uncertainty&#44; act with agency&#44; communicate signals&#44; and participate in environments where change is constant and often ambiguous&#46;
This session introduces Human Readiness and the VISIBLE&#8482; Framework &#8212; Voice&#44; Identity&#44; Spotlight&#44; Inner Work&#44; Belief&#44; Leverage&#44; and Elevation &#8212; as an applied lens for examining how confidence&#44; visibility&#44; identity&#44; trust&#44; and social context shape adaptive action&#46; Rather than presenting confidence as a personality trait&#44; the session frames it as a practical condition that influences participation&#44; belief updating&#44; decision&#45;making&#44; and collective learning&#46;
Participants will leave with a framework for identifying human readiness gaps in teams&#44; organizations&#44; learning environments&#44; and communities&#46;
Because adaptive systems require more than intelligence&#46; They require humans ready to perceive&#44; act&#44; and learn&#46;
By the end of this session&#44; participants will be able to&#58;
1&#46; Identify three Human Readiness gaps that may limit adaptive action&#44; including low agency&#44; uncertainty avoidance&#44; reduced voice&#44; identity threat&#44; low trust&#44; or lack of participation&#46;
2&#46; Apply the VISIBLE&#8482; Framework to examine how voice&#44; identity&#44; belief&#44; visibility&#44; confidence&#44; and social context influence human action under uncertainty&#46;
3&#46; Design one practical Human Readiness strategy that supports stronger agency&#44; clearer communication&#44; more adaptive learning&#44; and more participatory systems&#46;
