# An elementary form of agency at planetary&#45;scale

Presenter: Sergio Rubin

Contribution: Livestreamed panel or group session

Public ID: talk&#45;f068e195458bd69b

## Submitted abstract

The claim that climate homeorhesis&#8212;non&#45;equilibrium steady&#45;state dynamics that underlie Earth&#8217;s long&#45;term habitability&#8212;depends on an integrated biosphere&#8211;climate system capable of acting on its own is central to debates surrounding the Gaia hypothesis&#46; This paper presents a proof of concept suggesting a minimal form of planetary agency based on a simple biosphere&#8211;climate system model operating through active inference&#46; This means that the internal states of the system &#40;biosphere&#41; will appear to infer shifts in net incoming solar radiation &#40;external states&#41; through its boundary states&#44; i&#46;e&#46; by integrating past and current surface temperatures &#40;sensory states&#41; and actively changing greenhouse forcing &#40;active states&#41; to maintain climate trajectories within geophysiological bounds&#46; When surface temperature is altered by solar fluctuation changes&#44; the system adjusts greenhouse and albedo parameters to restore expected climate trajectories&#44; analogous to correcting sensory prediction errors in biological systems&#46; Perturbing active states &#40;e&#46;g&#46;&#44; imposed changes in albedo&#41; does not reduce inference precision&#46; Inference robustness decreases when the system&#8217;s degrees of freedom increase&#46; Active inference falls&#44; and thus climate homeorhesis fails when there is no biosphere activity &#40;lifeless planet&#41; or when it is decoupled from the climate system&#46; Predictive behaviour operates within the range of solar constants consistent with life&#8217;s presence on Earth &#40;&#126;&#8201;4 Ga to &#126;&#8201;1&#46;5 Ga expected in the future&#41;&#59; outside this range&#44; inference fails&#44; undermining a putative form of planetary agency&#46;
