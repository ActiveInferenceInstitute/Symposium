# CogNarr Story Graph&#58; A Formal Semantics for Narrative as a Belief Model

Presenter: John Boik&#44; Glen EP Ropella

Contribution: Livestreamed panel or group session

Public ID: talk&#45;414af3e53e96ece8

## Submitted abstract

People naturally express their beliefs about a topic&#44; situation&#44; or problem in the form of narrative&#46; CogNarr is a soon&#45;to&#45;be&#45;released&#44; open&#45;source&#44; interactive tool that lets a writer transform narrative into an explicit&#44; formal semantic graph&#44; which they can inspect and correct until it faithfully reflects their belief model&#46; A CogNarr story graph captures a narrative&#39;s argument structure&#44; its timing&#44; and the modality of its claims&#44; including predictions and conditionals&#44; together with the uncertainty attached to each&#46; In general it expresses the verifiable meaning of what a person wishes to say&#44; while reducing the ambiguity of natural language&#46; As such&#44; it could be of use in modeling the beliefs that active&#45;inference processes operate over&#44; and as a framework within which active&#45;inference methods could be employed to infer semantic meaning from text&#46; Because it makes a person&#39;s beliefs explicit&#44; transparent&#44; and shareable&#44; CogNarr has potential wherever consequential beliefs are expressed as narrative&#44; from medicine and governance to public polling and accident reporting&#46; As well&#44; CogNarr story graphs support accurate agent&#45;agent communication&#46; The collective&#45;cognition aim behind the project is alignment of shared belief models during decision&#45;making processes in the large&#45;group setting&#46; The talk will be a preview of features and capabilities&#44; with some discussion of potential applications&#46;
